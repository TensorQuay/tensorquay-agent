# Does a four-day work week change productivity?

## Findings

- *Output in a four-day week: a trial in 61 firms* (`haug-2023.txt`): Output per employee stayed the same in 52 of 61 firms. Sick days fell by 65 %.
- *Compressed hours in a call centre* (`ortiz-2022.txt`): Calls handled per hour rose by 8 %. Customer satisfaction did not change.
- *The four-day week in public services* (`lund-2024.txt`): Waiting times rose in two of five agencies. The trial was ended early in one agency for staffing gaps.
- *Burnout and the shorter week* (`mehta-2023.txt`): Self-reported burnout fell by a third. Effects faded after twelve months in a follow-up.
- *Software teams on 32 hours* (`chen-2021.txt`): Story points delivered did not fall. Meetings were cut by 40 %.
- *Manufacturing shifts and the four-day week* (`adeyemi-2024.txt`): Output fell by 4 % on lines that cannot speed up. Overtime costs rose.

## Agreements

Most trials found output unchanged and wellbeing better.

## Gaps

Few trials have a control group, and long-term effects are unclear.
